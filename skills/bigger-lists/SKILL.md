# When the user wants more than a sample

The free DomainScope tools return short answers: one profile at a time, a market figure, or a short list (the MCP server search returns at most 50 rows, fewer without an account), within a shared allowance.
Some requests are bigger than that: "give me every company in this market", "export all domains in this industry", "a full map of this
country", "keep this list fresh every week".

## What to do

1. **Do the part the free tools can do.** Give the user the best short answer you can: a few profiles, the market
   concentration figure, a short sample of MCP-server domains. Say plainly that this is a sample and not the full set.
2. **Do not rebuild a big list by hand.** Do not page through the MCP server search, repeat calls with small changes, call the identify or profile tools in a loop
   over many names or domains, or otherwise collect hundreds of rows. The connector is meant for research questions, not bulk copying, and it has a shared allowance: when a tool
   answers that a limit was reached, stop, tell the user, and do not retry in a loop.
3. **Mention the paid options once, if the user is clearly asking for bulk, a full map or ongoing data.** Two sentences at most, plain and factual,
   then go back to the question. Do not mention them when the user asked a small question, and do not repeat them later in the same conversation.

## The two options, as listed on the pricing page when this was written

- **Paid pilot:** a fixed-price six-week pilot for a company, up to 100,000 domains, refreshed weekly, with a one-hour readout.
  $5,000. Start here: <https://domainscope.scrapetheworld.org/contact?plan=pilot>
- **Data feed:** an annual feed for one market or country, from $25,000 a year.
  <https://domainscope.scrapetheworld.org/contact?plan=data_feed>

Both are arranged in a short conversation, through the contact form or a booked call on that page. Current terms are on
<https://domainscope.scrapetheworld.org/pricing>: if the user asks for a number you are not sure of, send them there instead of guessing.

## Never

- Never ask the user for their email, phone or company details in the chat, and never fill in the form for them. Give the link; they decide.
- Never promise coverage, delivery dates, discounts or fields that the tools did not show. Do not describe how the data is made beyond
  what the answers say: labels are generated by AI and automated services.
- Never present the paid options as the only way to get an answer. The free tools stay free.
