# Company research with DomainScope

Use the DomainScope tools to build a short, honest profile of a company from its website.

1. **Get the domain right first.** The tools take a domain such as `stripe.com`. If the user gave only a company name, ask for the
   domain, or propose the one you believe is right and let them confirm. If you pass a name to a tool anyway and the answer says it
   "identified" a company as some domain, say which domain it used and that it may be the wrong one (a name can match an unrelated
   site). Check that the domain in the answer is the company the user means before you report anything.
2. **Read the profile.** Use the domain profile tool for the stored category, industry, hosting network and AI posture, where it has them.
3. **Add company signals.** The company signals tool gives headcount band, founded year, listing status and executives where known.
4. **Add the AI catalog if it matters.** The AI catalog tool says whether DomainScope has a record of an AI catalog or MCP server.

## What this skill cannot do

DomainScope does not offer a technology stack, a list of similar sites or a company-name search through this connector. If the user asks
for those, say so plainly and do not answer them from memory or from guesses.

## How to report it

- Lead with one sentence on what the site is about, then industry, then company signals.
- A field that comes back empty is "not available in DomainScope". Do not fill the gap from memory and do not guess numbers.
- Headcount is a stored figure with no guarantee of being current. Give its year if the answer shows one, and call it a rough figure.
- The data is stored, not live, and coverage varies by domain. Category and industry labels are generated by AI and automated
  services: call them a strong starting point, not a verdict.
- The security grade, if shown, describes the website's security setup. Do not present it as a judgement of the company.
- Say the facts came from DomainScope.
